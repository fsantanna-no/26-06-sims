# Original vs new (26/10/06)

- original (tpd-21, consensus.eval): chat (Wikimedia IRC) and
  newsgroup (comp.compilers) on simulated peers
    - network: chat 5 peers syncing with 3 random peers after
      each message; news 15 peers with 5; no time: forks only
      from partial syncs
    - (a) storage: chain vs payload
    - (b) consensus: `list order` time, no init phase
    - (c) forks: 18% chat, 14% news ("a reasonable level of
      asynchrony", sizes not justified)
    - (d) friction: extra likes for newcomers, 3.7%
- new, single peer
    - more corpora and actions: SE (votes), wiki (reverts as
      revokes), GitHub (likes, dislikes, minimized comments),
      Lemmy (mod removals with reasons, restores, author
      deletes); the original had posts and likes only
    - moderation: revokes and reasons, recidivism, community
      vs dictator funding, vote affordability (GitHub: 70% of
      reactions unaffordable), sweep bytes reclaimed
    - cost at full scale: cost per action vs chain size, whole
      corpora; build matters (`adhd`: main 14h50m vs
      tree-trash 2h07m)
    - open vs gated: every poster begs first; `adhd`
      moderators never afford a revoke
- new, P2P (`260924-topology.md`)
    - topology: hubs-59 (5 supers, 9 mids, 45 leaves, 90
      links) from measured super-peer networks, not random
      peers
    - sync in chain time: push with relay delay (chat
      instant, others 60 s), from ActivityPub and Bitcoin
      trickling, not "sync after each message"
    - forks as an OUTCOME per corpus from real gaps (chat
      ~3%, others ~4-19% expected), not a fixed 14-18%
    - convergence: same `list order` and `reps` on all 59;
      time to reach all peers
    - partitions (planned): mids cut for 1 or 8 days (> 
      `time.fork`): hard forks and recovery
    - a real partition: Lemmy one-way block (lemmy.dbzer0.com
      -> feddit.org) on two peers (`261003-lemmy.md`)
- found along the way: concurrency bugs in freechains (lost
  actions under parallel sends), sync cost, data artefacts
  (usenet moderation batches, date-only records)
